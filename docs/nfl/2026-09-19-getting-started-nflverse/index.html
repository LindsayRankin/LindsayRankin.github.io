Getting Started with NFL Data in R

Why?

Watching my 2025 Chicago Bears, I thought our QB Caleb Williams protected the ball well, throwing very few interceptions. And when he was picked off, a number of them were on 4th downs: When a short interception (with little return) is not much different than an incompletion or failed conversion, and when a long pass may be nearly like a punt or at least better than an incompletion (scenarios when the defensive backs should really bat the ball down for the sake of their team’s field positions, but they often can’t resist).

I wondered what QB metrics might look like if 4th down interceptions were treated differently. I didn’t know anything at the time about NFL data or metrics to represent QB play (beyond pure interception counts) - but months later I saw someone had looked at a pretty similar question to mine:

Awesome! 🤯

So I wanted to dive in, figure out if I could get some NFL data, and ask and answer my own future questions.

I’m starting off just documenting (for myself, and anyone else who stumbles here some day) how I jumped in to getting familiar with some NFL data. I wasn’t interested in data for fantasy football and so much of it seemed to be behind paywalls, but I wanted to figure out what was out there:
Would NFL data of some kind be easily and freely (or affordably) available to jump into asking questions, or am I going to have to spend a bunch of time figuring out web scraping and then cleaning the data before being able to ask and answer any questions?
For the most part, it’s the former!

nflverse Data

There is an nflverse group of R packages and NFL data, that conforms data to tidy styles, includes play-by-play data with some pre-processing, has a nifty ggplot2-compatible graphing package to to easily incorporate team colors and logos, and is updated as the season goes. Amazing 😍

Getting started in nflverse

There are some short getting-started guides from the nflverse, but I found the big picture of nflverse and all the different options a bit confusing at first.

For example, what is the difference of nflfastR vs nflreadr to get play-by-play data?
The nflfastR package can scrape play-by-play data and calculate advanced metrics. This could be useful if you want to add or modify metric calculations or the samples they are applied to. But that might be a more complicated future endeavor.
But the easier starting point is the nflreadr package, which lets you download data sets from Github, including play-by-play data with a lot of metrics pre-calculated and included.

Introduction to NFL Analytics with R by Brad Congelio was the most helpful resource to get started. A free, online book focusing on nlfverse packages. Highly recommend, and huge props and thanks to the author! 🙌

I already knew R, so I was able to skip around the chapters. But the book started with some good context to understand the landscape of NFL data and analytics and the nflverse, and gave me some structure to jumping into the data by using and modifying his examples.

I have a couple other sites bookmarked, and if I dive into them and find them helpful I’ll update this and add them here.

Other NFL data options

One final find to note was stumbling on this blog post which gave an overview of the lay of the land in public-facing NFL data and analytics from the consumer perspective. There are a lot of sites trying to essentially create interactive dashboards, and a lot of it is geared toward fantasy football. But as someone who wants to access the data directly and has the coding background for custom analyses and visualization, a lot of that isn’t of interest to me. So I’ll be stick with free nflverse data for now, but sounds like there are some interesting options out there (for data itself as well, not just shiny interaction tools) if this becomes a 💸💸💸 kind of hobby ️😂
